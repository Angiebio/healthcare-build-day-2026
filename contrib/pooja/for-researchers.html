For computational researchers

You are the primary user. The question this answers is the one that currently costs you months: does a cohort exist that fits my method, and is it worth pursuing?

The problem we are actually solving for you

Finding out whether suitable data exists should not require a data use agreement. Today it often does. You approach institutions one at a time, describe a study you cannot yet scope, and wait for approvals that tell you only whether you may look, not whether looking is worthwhile.

Lantern separates the two. Discovery is open and governed. Access is petitioned and audited. You can size a cohort, inspect its distribution, and decide against it entirely without anyone signing anything.

The search axis that does not exist elsewhere

Clinical measurements are recorded in prose. A radiologist writes that the lateral ventricular atrial width measures 12.4 mm, and that number becomes unreachable, because search engines index words rather than quantities.

We compile those values into typed facts at the hospital, so you can query them directly.

You can now askWhich today requires
Fetal atrial width above 10 mmReading every report by hand
Ejection fraction below 40 percentA site-by-site chart review
Severe cases only, above 15 mmA query no interface can express

Thresholds are ordinary comparisons, so stratification works the way you would expect. Mild, moderate, and severe are a range query rather than three separate keyword hunts.

Every number carries its evidence

A measurement arrives with three things attached: the confidence our extractor assigned, the provenance of the fact, and the exact sentence it was taken from. You do not have to trust the parser. You can read the phrase and decide in about two seconds.

This matters more than it sounds. An extracted value with no traceable source is not a scientific observation, it is a rumour with a decimal point. Ours are auditable individually, which is what makes the axis usable in work you intend to publish.

A machine-derived value is always visibly machine-derived. It never appears in the same register as a documented diagnosis.

What the corpus cannot support, stated plainly

Every passport carries a field naming its own limits. For this dataset that includes acquisition parameters and pixel data, because the supplied corpus contains neither. There is no field strength, no slice geometry, no reconstruction kernel, and no image.

So we index no image-derived features and claim no similarity search. If your method needs voxel spacing or diffusion directions, this index will tell you immediately that it cannot help, which is worth more to you than a hopeful maybe.

When a cohort is too small to disclose

Rare presentations are where the science is and where the risk is. When a query matches fewer studies than the disclosure threshold, that hospital returns a count band and a petition route rather than records.

The threshold is returned in the response, so you always know the rule being applied to you. Suppression happens per institution, so one hospital withholding does not prevent the others from answering.

Getting the underlying data

Petition through the interface with your institution, IRB reference, and stated purpose. The request routes to the hospital that owns the studies and they decide. Approval is recorded in an append-only audit trail alongside the request that prompted it.

We never hold source images, so we are never the party deciding whether you receive them. That is deliberate. The institution carrying the obligation is the institution making the call.
