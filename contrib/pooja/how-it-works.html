The compiler

Every hospital node runs a privacyâ€“utility compiler over its own studies. The compiler runs inside the hospital's boundary; nothing downstream ever sees its input. It performs five operations in order, and the order matters:

  1. Strip direct identifiers. Patient name, medical record number, birth date, and institution are removed or replaced with a consistent salted pseudonym.
  2. Extract measurements from prose. Quantitative clinical values are parsed out of the report into typed, structured facts: a value, a unit, an anatomical quantity, and the exact source snippet.
  3. Code the findings. Clinical concepts are mapped to standard vocabularies so a search for one term reaches equivalent terms at other institutions.
  4. Generalize what remains. Exact birth date becomes a developmental stage and an age band. Exact study dates are shifted consistently, preserving intervals while destroying the calendar.
  5. Stamp provenance. Every field records where it came from.

The output is a Study Passport. The input, including the full report text, never leaves.

Why extraction before discard is the whole idea

The intuitive approach to privacy is to delete the risky field. But a radiology report is both the riskiest field and the most scientifically valuable one. Deleting it protects the patient and destroys the research value; keeping it does the reverse.

Compiling resolves the dilemma. We compute the useful facts while still inside the boundary, where the sensitive text is already lawfully held, and then discard the text. What crosses the boundary is a number with a unit and a source, not a sentence that might name a school, a parent, or a hometown.

The measurement was always there. It was just written in a form that no search could reach and no privacy office could release.

Search

A query, typed in natural language or built with filters, is compiled into a validated query structure before anything executes. This is a deliberate security boundary: a language model may propose an interpretation, but only deterministic validation disposes. An unrecognized field, an unsupported operator, or an out-of-range value is rejected outright rather than quietly adjusted. A silently dropped filter would be a privacy failure, not an inconvenience.

The validated query fans out to every hospital node in parallel. Each node evaluates it against its own records and its own release policy, and returns only what its policy permits. Results are combined using rank fusion across independent signals (vocabulary match, measured values, and text relevance), so that no single signal can dominate and the ranking degrades gracefully if one is unavailable.

Every result explains itself

A result never arrives as a bare score. It carries the reasons it matched, and each underlying fact is labeled with its origin: read directly from a structured field, extracted from the report with a stated confidence, or curated by a human.

This distinction is not decorative. In clinical research the difference between a documented diagnosis and a software inference is the difference between evidence and a hypothesis. A system that blurs the two is worse than useless. So a machine-derived value in Lantern is always visibly machine-derived, and the sentence it came from is always available for a human to check.

Access

Discovery ends where authorization begins. When a researcher needs more than the passport, they submit a petition capturing their identity, institution, IRB reference, and purpose. It routes to the hospital that owns the data.

Lantern does not hold source images and does not serve them. That capability is architecturally absent rather than merely restricted by policy, a distinction that matters, because a system cannot misuse a capability it does not have. Every petition and every decision is appended to an audit trail with no update or delete path.

What we did not do

The dataset behind this demonstration contains study metadata and radiology reports. It contains no pixel data and no acquisition parameters. Accordingly, Lantern indexes no image-derived features and claims no image analysis. Where a capability would require data this corpus does not contain, the passport says so explicitly, in a field listing what is missing for full computability.

Naming the gap is part of the design. A researcher deciding whether to spend six months on a cohort is better served by an honest inventory than an impressive one.
