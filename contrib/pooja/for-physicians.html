For physicians

You are looking at an uncommon presentation and want to know whether anyone else has seen one like it. That question is currently answered by who you happen to know.

Finding comparable cases across institutions

Rare findings are rare locally and less rare nationally. A colleague three hospitals away may have managed four of them. Today the way you discover that is a conference corridor, a mailing list, or luck.

Lantern lets you search the participating network by finding, anatomy, developmental stage, and by the measured values themselves. If comparable studies exist, you will see where, and how many, without anyone disclosing a patient.

The line we hold, and why you should want us to hold it

What this system returns is research discovery. It is not a second opinion, not a diagnostic aid, and not clinical evidence about your patient.

That distinction is enforced in the interface rather than left to your judgment under time pressure. Values extracted from reports are labelled as extracted, carry a confidence, and display the sentence they came from. Nothing here is presented as a validated clinical finding, because nothing here has been validated as one.

A tool that blurs research similarity into clinical evidence is worse than no tool, because it produces confident answers in exactly the situations where confidence is unearned.

What you see that a researcher does not

Clinician access sits one tier above researcher access. In practice that means narrower developmental banding where it is clinically meaningful, and a direct route to the institution holding a study rather than a generic petition queue.

Access is decided by role on the server. Nothing in the browser can grant you more than your role permits, which also means nothing in the browser can be manipulated into granting it.

Requesting the underlying study

If you need more than a passport, you submit a request that names your institution, your purpose, and, where applicable, your treatment relationship. It routes to the hospital that owns the study, and a person there decides.

There is no automatic cross-institution transfer of source data, for treatment purposes or any other. Every request and every decision is written to an append-only audit trail. If you are ever asked to account for how you obtained a study, the record exists and it is not editable, including by us.

Why the counts sometimes refuse to sharpen

When a query narrows to a very small group, the system returns a band rather than an exact number, and withholds the records. This will occasionally frustrate you, and it is working correctly.

In a rare paediatric presentation, an exact count can identify a child. The threshold that triggers this is shown to you in the response rather than hidden, and the petition route stays open. You are not blocked from the data. You are asked to request it from the people accountable for it.
